{% comment %} ADR-0269. We replace the software that processes our customer's customers' data, on our own initiative and without asking. Art. 28.3.a GDPR has the processor acting on the controller's documented instructions, so if nothing says that keeping the system current is part of the instructed service, every release is processing nobody documented. The second half matters just as much: art. 28.2 gives the controller a right to object to a NEW sub-processor. Replacing our own software adds no recipient and moves no data, so it does not open that right, and saying so is what stops the two from being confused, in either direction. Section 5 (sub-processors) keeps its own regime untouched. {% endcomment %}

{% trans "10. Maintenance and updating of the system" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} ERPlora keeps and updates the software that processes the data on its own initiative, without the customer having to request it. This forms part of the service the customer contracts and is one of the security measures required by article 32 of the GDPR, in particular the ongoing integrity, availability, and resilience of the processing systems and the regular evaluation of the measures in place. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} An update replaces the software, not the data: it does not change the purposes of the processing, the categories of data, the retention periods, or the recipients, and no data is copied outside the customer's Hub in order to carry it out. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} For that reason an update is not a change of sub-processor and does not open the right to object provided for in article 28.2 of the GDPR. That right is unaffected for the processors listed in section 5: any addition or replacement among them is notified in advance, and the customer may object. {% endblocktrans %}
