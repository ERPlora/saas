{% comment %} Section 7 is written against three sources, not invented: - Art. 85.3 TRLGDCU — a clause reserving unilateral modification is ABUSIVE, and therefore void, "salvo que concurran motivos válidos especificados en el contrato". Hence 7.2: the list of reasons is not decoration, it is what keeps the rest of the section standing. - Arts. 126 and 126 bis TRLGDCU (= art. 19 of Directive (EU) 2019/770) — a modification BEYOND what conformity requires needs a valid reason, no extra cost, clear information, and, when it hurts access or use more than minimally, advance notice ON A DURABLE MEDIUM plus 30 calendar days to walk away free of charge. 126 bis.4 would let us skip the termination right by offering the unmodified version — we cannot: the fleet runs one version (ADR-0269). So the right is real and it is granted. - Art. 8.2/8.3 of the same Directive — we must supply the updates needed to stay in conformity, and we are shielded from a fault caused solely by an update the customer chose not to install, but only if we announced it AND its consequences. That is 7.7. Those articles bind us for CONSUMERS. Nearly every customer is a business and therefore is not one, but we cannot classify them reliably at signup and the promise is the same either way, so the section grants the regime to everybody (7.5, last paragraph). Not legal advice and not written by a lawyer: pm#69 carries the list of what a professional has to check before this is relied on. {% endcomment %}
